### DoltLite in the Dolt Workbench

It makes me sad that more people don't know about [the Dolt Workbench](https://github.com/dolthub/dolt-workbench). With agent mode, the Dolt Workbench becomes [Cursor for SQL](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-02-09-introducing-agent-mode/). You can use it with any MySQL-, Postgres-, and [now SQLite-compatible database](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-21-dolt-workbench-supports-doltlite/). But it really shines when used with the Dolt versions of those products.

Give it a spin with [a DoltLite database](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-21-dolt-workbench-supports-doltlite/), no server required. Let an agent do the querying. It's magic.

### Postgres Transactions

If you've been away for the summer, you may have missed that [Doltgres](https://www.doltgres.com), the Postgres-compatible flavor of Dolt, [went 1.0](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-06-doltgres-1-0/). We haven't stopped working on compatibility, and there is nothing like a launch to have users scare bugs out from under their rocks. As part of that compatibility work, we've been matching some weird behavior with implicit transactions. [Zach wrote about those this week](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-26-postgres-implicit-transactions/).

### Prolly Trees in ACM

Continuing with this week's academic theme, we love when people write about [Prolly Trees](https://www.dolthub.com/docs/architecture/storage-engine/prolly-tree/), the novel data structure at the core of Dolt. You can even get a good feel for how Prolly Trees work with [the new Prolly Tree visualizer](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-10-prolly-tree-visualizer/) available at my latest vanity purchased domain [prollytree.com](https://www.prollytree.com). 

[Nick reviewed a Prolly Tree paper](https://www.dolthub.com/blog/2026-08-24-evaluating-research-papers-in-the-age-of-ai/) published in an ACM journal this week. Nothing too novel in there but we still love to see people engage with our favorite data structure.
